# CS-250-Software-Development-Lifecycle
Contains coursework and portfolio artifacts from CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle at SNHU
## Course Overview

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user is trying to accomplish and then turning those needs into clear requirements that the development team can work with. User stories help with this because they describe a feature from the user's perspective instead of only focusing on the technical work required to build it. Acceptance criteria can then define what needs to happen for that story to be considered complete. This gives developers and testers a shared understanding of what they are building and why it matters to the user.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by breaking a larger problem into smaller pieces that can be developed, tested, and improved over time. Agile supports this approach by allowing the team to respond to feedback and changing requirements instead of assuming the original plan will never change. In future development work, I would use practices such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, backlog refinement, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives. These processes keep the team focused while still giving them opportunities to communicate, adjust priorities, and continuously improve how they work.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for your work, and understanding how your work affects the rest of the team. Developers, testers, Product Owners, and Scrum Masters have different responsibilities, but they still depend on one another to reach the same goal. A good team member should raise problems early, ask questions instead of making assumptions, be open to feedback, and help the team adapt when something changes. Software development is collaborative, so individual technical ability only goes so far if the team cannot communicate and work together effectively.
